Mac screenshots are named with a date and a time, like `Screenshot 2026-11-12 at 9.41.07 PM.png`. That is fine for sorting and no help when you are trying to remember what was in one. Here are three ways to get to the one you need.

## Method 1: Finder, with the date you remember

1. Open Finder and go to the folder where screenshots are saved. On a new Mac that is the **Desktop**.
2. Press <kbd class="key">⌘</kbd> <kbd class="key">F</kbd>, then set **Kind** to **Image** to hide everything that is not a picture.
3. Sort by **Date Created** and scroll to the day you took it.

If you are not sure which folder to look in, press <kbd class="key">⇧</kbd> <kbd class="key">⌘</kbd> <kbd class="key">5</kbd>, open **Options**, and read the **Save to** line.

## Method 2: Spotlight, for words in the name

Spotlight finds files by name and other details. Screenshots rarely have a name that says anything, so searching `Screenshot October` lists captures from that month and not much more. Whether Spotlight also finds words inside an image depends on your macOS version and settings, so try it with a word you remember before you count on it.

## Method 3: Search the words inside, with Shotlight

1. Install Shotlight and let it watch your screenshot folder.
2. In setup, choose **Only make searchable** to index the old ones without renaming them.
3. Press <kbd class="key">⌥</kbd> <kbd class="key">Space</kbd> and type what was on the screen, like `uber receipt`.

Shotlight reads each screenshot on your Mac, so the text in the image, the app and the website are all searchable, and nothing is uploaded.

## What this does not cover

Shotlight reads screenshots in the folders you choose. It cannot bring back a screenshot you already deleted, and it does not search your Photos library. On macOS 15 the Finder steps are the same.
