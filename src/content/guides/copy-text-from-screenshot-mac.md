A screenshot is a picture, but the words in it are often what you want. You can copy them without typing anything again. Macs read text in images with a feature called Live Text, and an app like Shotlight can keep that text so you can search it later.

| Method | Works on | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Preview | One image at a time | Copying a paragraph or a number right now |
| Quick Look and Photos | One image at a time | A fast look, with no app to open |
| Shotlight | Every screenshot in your folders | Finding a screenshot from a word, then copying its text |

## Method 1: Preview

1. Double-click the screenshot in Finder. It opens in **Preview**.
2. Move the pointer over the text. The pointer turns into a text cursor when it is over words.
3. Drag across the words to select them, then press <kbd class="key">⌘</kbd> <kbd class="key">C</kbd>.
4. Paste the text into any app with <kbd class="key">⌘</kbd> <kbd class="key">V</kbd>.

## Method 2: Quick Look and Photos

Select the screenshot in Finder and press <kbd class="key">Space</kbd> to open Quick Look. On recent versions of macOS you can select text right in that window. The Photos app works the same way, if the screenshot is in your Photos library.

## Method 3: Keep the text searchable, with Shotlight

Live Text works on one image at a time, so it is no use when you remember a word and have no idea which screenshot it was in. [Shotlight](/what-is-shotlight) reads every screenshot in the folders you choose and keeps the text on your Mac.

1. Press <kbd class="key">⌥</kbd> <kbd class="key">Space</kbd> and type a word you remember.
2. Open the result in the Library.
3. Find the text Shotlight read from the image and choose **Copy all**.

Because the text is saved, a search for `invoice number` can find the screenshot that shows one, even when its file name says nothing. That is the quickest way to [find an old screenshot on Mac](/guides/find-old-screenshots-mac) when you only remember a word. Shotlight can also [rename each screenshot](/guides/rename-screenshots-mac) from the text it reads. Shotlight reads screenshots with Apple's own text recognition on your Mac, and it blanks anything that looks like a password or a key before it stores a word. It is free while in early access, and the [download page](/download) has the install steps.

## Tips for cleaner results

- Zoom in before you select. Larger text is read more accurately than a small thumbnail of a page.
- Select a line or a short block at a time. A long selection across columns can paste in an odd order.
- Paste into a plain text field, or use <kbd class="key">⌥</kbd> <kbd class="key">⇧</kbd> <kbd class="key">⌘</kbd> <kbd class="key">V</kbd> to paste without the source formatting, so the text takes the style of the document you are in.
- Take screenshots at full size. A screenshot of a shrunken window has smaller text and gives worse results.

## When the text comes out wrong

Text recognition struggles with small type, low contrast, handwriting and stylized fonts. If a result looks wrong, zoom in on the image and select again, or take the screenshot again at a larger size. Always read what you pasted before you send it on, especially numbers.
